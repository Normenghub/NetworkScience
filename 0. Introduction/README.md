# Introduction to Networks

This document outlines the fundamental concepts and types of 'networks' that define the world we live in. In essence, a network is the simplest description of a set of interconnected entities, which are called 'nodes', and their connections, which are called 'links'. It is a general yet powerful way to represent and study both simple and complex interactions.

## 1. Social Networks
* A social network is a group of people (nodes) connected by relationships (links) such as friendship or collaboration.
* Health workers analyze them to combat the spread of diseases, economists use them to address labor market inequality, and scientists study coauthorship networks to identify influential thinkers.
* Platforms like Facebook allow us to study human interactions at a scale and resolution that was never possible in the past.
* However, there are privacy risks, such as predicting sexual orientation based on online friends or impersonating friends to steal sensitive information (social phishing).
* Other data sources include mapping human mobility via cell phones or finding communities in movie star co-star networks using IMDB data.

## 2. Communication Networks
* In networks like Twitter, links are not necessarily reciprocal (you can follow someone without them following you back).
* A retweet network of political messages shows a strong pattern of 'homophily', where conservative users mostly retweet other conservatives, and progressives share progressive content.
* These networks can be manipulated by malicious entities using social bots and fake news sites to amplify disinformation campaigns and manipulate opinions.
* Another communication network example is email (e.g., the Enron dataset), which features directed links from senders to receivers.

## 3. The Web and The Internet
* The Web is an information network originally created by Tim Berners-Lee using a naming system (URL), a simple language (HTML), and a transfer protocol (HTTP).
* In the Wikipedia network of math articles, an article's importance can be measured by 'PageRank', and nodes often form tightly connected clusters around specific topics.
* The physical Internet is a "network of networks" connected by special nodes called routers.
* It features a hierarchical core-periphery structure, with a dense, highly redundant backbone carrying the most traffic, and a more sparsely connected periphery.

## 4. Transportation Networks
* Road networks evolve locally to minimize the distance traveled between nearby cities, leading to the emergence of grid-like structures.
* In contrast, air transportation networks display a "hub and spoke" structure.
* Airlines minimize hops between source and destination by connecting airports to existing hubs, rather than adding costly direct flights between low-traffic airports.

## 5. Biological Networks
* Inside our cells, proteins interact to form protein interaction networks, metabolic networks, and gene regulatory networks.
* At a higher level, connections between neural cells (synapses) create the neural networks in our brains.
* At the species level, animals interact to form ecological networks or food webs. Removing a node (e.g., when a species goes extinct) affects the survival of other parts of the ecosystem network.
